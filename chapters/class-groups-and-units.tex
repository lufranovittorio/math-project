\chapter{Class Groups and Units}\label{class-groups-and-units}
\chapterstatus{planned}

The two finiteness theorems of algebraic number theory, proved with the geometry of numbers: the class group of a number field is finite, and its unit group is finitely generated.

\noindent\textbf{Prerequisites.} Chapter~\ref{algebraic-number-theory} (Algebraic Number Theory), Chapter~\ref{geometry-of-numbers} (Geometry of Numbers) and Chapter~\ref{continued-fractions} (Continued Fractions and Diophantine Approximation).

\noindent\textbf{Planned contents.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Minkowski embedding of a number field; the ring of integers and its ideals as lattices, and their covolumes.
\item The Minkowski bound; finiteness of the class number; computation of class groups of quadratic and cubic fields.
\item Minkowski's theorem that $|d_K| > 1$ for $K \neq \QQ$; Hermite's theorem on fields of bounded discriminant.
\item Dirichlet's unit theorem and the regulator; units of real quadratic fields and the Pell equation; units of cubic fields.
\item The Dedekind zeta function and the analytic class number formula.
\item Exercises with solutions.
\end{enumerate}
