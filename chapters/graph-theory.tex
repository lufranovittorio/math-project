\chapter{Graph Theory}\label{graph-theory}
\chapterstatus{planned}

Graphs model relations between finitely many objects. This chapter covers the structural theory of
graphs, from trees and connectivity to matchings, colourings and planarity, and ends with Ramsey theory and
the spectra of graphs.

\noindent\textbf{Prerequisites.} Chapter~\ref{combinatorics} (Combinatorics) and Chapter~\ref{linear-algebra} (Linear Algebra).

\noindent\textbf{Planned contents.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Graphs, subgraphs, walks, connectivity, bipartite graphs; degree sequences and the handshake lemma.
\item Trees: characterisations, spanning trees, Cayley's formula, the matrix-tree theorem.
\item Eulerian and Hamiltonian graphs: Euler's theorem, Dirac's and Ore's theorems.
\item Matchings: Hall's marriage theorem, K\H{o}nig's theorem, Tutte's theorem; stable matchings (Gale--Shapley).
\item Connectivity and flows: Menger's theorem, max-flow min-cut.
\item Colourings: Brooks' theorem, chromatic polynomial, Vizing's theorem, perfect graphs (overview).
\item Planar graphs: Euler's formula, Kuratowski's theorem, the five colour theorem; the four colour theorem (statement).
\item Extremal graph theory: Mantel and Tur\'an; Ramsey theory: Ramsey's theorem, bounds for $R(k,k)$, van der Waerden (statement).
\item Spectral graph theory: adjacency and Laplacian matrices, expanders, the Cheeger inequality.
\item Exercises with solutions.
\end{enumerate}
