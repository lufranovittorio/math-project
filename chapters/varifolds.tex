\section{Introduction}\label{varifolds:section-introduction}

Integral currents (Chapter~\ref{currents}) solve Plateau's problem for oriented surfaces, but soap films are not oriented and are not always mass minimising: a M\"obius strip of soap is a stable film, and
the triple junctions of soap films, where three sheets meet at angles of $120^\circ$, are invisible to currents, because orientations cannot be chosen consistently around the junction. \emph{Varifolds},
introduced by Almgren and developed by Allard, are measures on the space of positions and unoriented tangent planes; they record area and tangent planes but no orientation, and their first variation
expresses the stationarity of area under all deformations. This chapter will develop varifolds from the definition to the monotonicity formula, with the compactness and regularity theorems of Allard
stated with references \cite{simon-gmt}.

\noindent\textbf{Prerequisites.} Chapter~\ref{currents} (Currents), Chapter~\ref{riemannian-geometry} (Riemannian Geometry).

\noindent\textbf{Planned contents.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Grassmannian of unoriented $k$-planes, varifolds as Radon measures on $U \times G(n, k)$, weight measure, push-forward by smooth maps, weak convergence and compactness.
\item Rectifiable and integral varifolds; the varifold of a submanifold; the limit of oscillating curves as a non-rectifiable varifold.
\item The first variation, the divergence on a plane, the first variation formula for submanifolds with mean curvature and boundary terms; stationary varifolds; examples: crossing lines, triple junctions,
cones.
\item The monotonicity formula with complete proof, existence and upper semicontinuity of densities, consequences for the support.
\item Allard's compactness theorem for integral varifolds and Allard's regularity theorem (statements, with references); the comparison with currents and the existence of stationary varifolds by
min-max (Almgren--Pitts), as a survey.
\end{enumerate}
